Harbor Family Medical: five homepage directions

One fictional family medicine practice, five genuinely different design systems. Same brand, same services, same providers, same trust signals — so the only variable is the design. Every concept is mobile-first with a distinct phone layout, not a collapsed desktop grid.

The five demos

1 · Harbor & Oak

Calm & premium · editorial serif

Hairline rules instead of cards. Numbered editorial service list, oversized serif pull quotes, and a great deal of whitespace.

  • Full-screen serif overlay menu on mobile
  • Sticky book bar appears only after scroll
  • Zero card grids anywhere
Open demo

2 · Harbor Bright

Bright & family-friendly · rounded sans

Pillowy cards, curved section dividers, blob shapes, and an age-band strip from babies to seniors.

  • Bottom tab bar on mobile — app-like, Book always visible
  • Services & doctors become horizontal snap carousels
  • Reviews drawn as speech bubbles
Open demo

3 · Harbor Connect

Modern tech-enabled · crisp grotesque + mono

Product-style UI where the hero is a working scheduler. Tabbed service channels, provider roster with next-available chips.

  • Functional slot picker (14 days, real state)
  • Live insurance search filter
  • Open/closed computed from posted hours
Open demo

4 · Harbor Commons

Community neighborhood · warm serif, photo-driven

Asymmetric photo mosaic hero, a "since 2004" timeline, and a community bulletin board of pinned notes.

  • Phone-first CTA hierarchy — Call is primary, Book secondary
  • Providers as neighbor rows with years-in-neighborhood
  • Mosaic collapses to one lead photo on mobile
Open demo

5 · Harbor Now

Minimal same-day hybrid · heavy uppercase sans

The hero is a live status board: open/closed, slots left today, wait time, next routine opening. Plus an honest "go to the ER instead" list.

  • Status panel jumps above the headline on phones
  • Permanent dual-action bar — Call + Book, always
  • Slots expire against the real clock
Open demo

Strongest for…

Highest trust 1 · Harbor & Oak Editorial restraint reads as competence. Long-form bios and pull quotes do the persuading; nothing shouts.
Best mobile conversion 5 · Harbor Now Live availability answers "can I be seen today" before any scrolling, and the dual-action bar never leaves the screen.
Best for families 2 · Harbor Bright Age bands, friendly tone, and swipe carousels built for one-handed browsing while holding a child.
Best telehealth positioning 3 · Harbor Connect Telehealth is a first-class tab and a toggle in the booking widget, not a footnote in a services list.

Open the estimate

Before any of this goes live for a real practice.
  • Every provider, review, phone number, address, price, and availability figure is fictional placeholder content and must be replaced with verified information from the practice.
  • Insurance lists create real billing complaints when wrong — they must come from the practice's contracts, not from a template.
  • No concept collects clinical data on the page. Booking is described as handing off to a HIPAA-compliant patient portal, and each demo carries a visible privacy notice. Keep that architecture: a plain web form emailing symptoms is not HIPAA-compliant.
  • The booking widget in Concept 3 and the status board in Concept 5 use deterministic demo logic, not a real scheduling system. Wire them to the practice's actual EHR/scheduler before launch.
  • Photography is represented by duotone placeholders. Real patient or staff photos require signed releases.
